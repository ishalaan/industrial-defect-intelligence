NEU-CLS: DATASET PROVENANCE AND LOCAL INTEGRITY RECORD
===================================================
Verification date: 27 September 2026

Source attribution and scope
----------------------------
The original NEU surface defect database is attributed to Kechen Song and
Yunhui Yan at Northeastern University. The university page describes 1,800
greyscale images, 200 x 200 pixels, comprising 300 examples in each of six
classes: crazing, inclusion, patches, pitted surface, rolled-in scale and
scratches. It distinguishes image-only NEU-CLS from annotated NEU-DET.

Original source, consulted for this audit:
https://faculty.neu.edu.cn/songkc/en/zdylm/263265/list/

Original reference requested by that source:
Song, K. and Yan, Y. (2013). A noise robust method based on completed local
binary patterns for hot-rolled steel strip surface defects. Applied Surface
Science, 285, pp. 858-864.

The project performs classification among the six represented defect classes.
There is no normal-steel class. Results do not establish general defect
detection or generalisation to other factories, cameras or steel grades.

Defensible conclusion and remaining limitations
-----------------------------------------------
The local experimental images are unchanged from the frozen manifest and
are byte-for-byte consistent, by SHA-256, with the retained ZIP entries.
The ZIP matches the file size and MD5 published for Figshare version 1.
This supports identifying the local copy with that Figshare distribution,
subject to the strength and scope of the checksum evidence stated above.

No claim is made that the Figshare package is byte-for-byte identical to
the separately hosted original Northeastern University archive. The
university page supplies the original description and attribution, but no
archive checksum was identified on that page. The university archive was
not retrieved or compared in this audit. The history of repackaging from
the original release to the Figshare directory and annotation structure
remains unverified. The original download date and download receipt are
not established by the available local records; filesystem timestamps are
not treated as proof of acquisition.

Final consolidation should cite this record, preserve these qualifications,
and use the recorded download URL, local archive SHA-256 and image manifest
to specify reproducible input checks. Safe download/extraction and a future
empty-folder reproduction are separate consolidation work. They were not
executed during this documentation-only remediation. Historical provenance
warnings in frozen code and saved outputs remain evidence of their original
execution context; this record supplies the current verification findings.
